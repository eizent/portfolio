## Redesigning the Accessibility Basics Course for Enhanced Student Learning and Engagement

Grace Cipressi, the Assistive Technology Specialist at Bryn Mawr, had designed an Accessibility Basics course for all incoming freshman to take as a part of orientation. 
However, the pilot launch of the program in the summer of 2025 was not as successful; many students did not complete the course and/or failed the final assignment to create an accessible recipe as a Word document. As a result, 
our team had to do a lot of manual grading, feedback, and regrading of the final assignment until they were able to pass. 

The original course content consisted of informative YouTube videos on accessibility and disability advocacy, web resources, like the WebAIM Contrast Checker, and a tutorial recorded on Panopto on how to make an accessible document, in addition to two discussions and a final submission. 
While the materials were well curated and informative, there was a lack of engagement with the materials from the students which caused them to make errors in writing alt text, proper heading structure, or other parts of the assignment guidelines.

![Screenshot from the original Accessibility Basics course](/assets/images/AB_original.png)

In order to increase student engagement and grasp of the material, I used the original content materials but presented them through Moodle Lessons. These Lessons allowed me to present the existing videos (which were embedded for full functionality),
content pages, and podcasts in a structured and guided way. Additionally, I created knowledge checks, or quick multiple choice and true/false questions, within the lesson to reinforce the lecture content. I set these questions up so 
students could not continue the lesson unless they had correctly answered. However, for each incorrect response, I used Generative AI to help me created detailed feedback to immediately give students t help guide them.
